THE TURNING POINT

A decades-long trend, decisively reversed

At the end of the last century, China's sandified land was still expanding. Today, on the very same land, it is shrinking — and shrinking faster. This is the real watershed in the history of desert control.

Late 20th century · Desertification expanding
+515k mu / year
Sandified land was expanding by 5.15 million mu a year. Sand advanced and people retreated; farmland and villages were swallowed by drifting dunes.
Today · Desertification reversing
−1000k mu / year
Sandified land is now shrinking by 10 million mu a year — a historic shift from "sand advancing" to "green advancing."
Net expansion has turned into net reduction — and the pace of reduction is roughly twice the old pace of expansion
Note on units: 1 mu ≈ 1/15 hectare, so 10 million mu ≈ 667,000 hectares; 1.52 million mu ≈ 101,300 hectares. The eight major deserts and four major sandy lands together account for the bulk of China's sandified area.

According to the National Forestry and Grassland Administration: China's sandified land area has declined for four consecutive monitoring periods, making China the first country to reach the 2030 land degradation neutrality target. Total soil wind erosion across the eight major deserts and four major sandy lands has fallen about 40% since 2000; average vegetation cover in sandy areas has reached 21.17%, up 2.84 percentage points from a decade ago.

TIMELINE

Generations of time, planted into the sand

China's desert control is not a single campaign, but a relay race spanning more than half a century.

1978
The Three-North Shelterbelt Program is launched
A vast ecological project spanning northwest, north and northeast China breaks ground, covering the country's most severely sandified regions and becoming one of the largest ecological engineering efforts in the world.
1984 — 1985
Farmer-led desert control takes root
Shi Guangyin founds China's first farmer shareholding sand-control company, contracting over 3,000 mu of barren sand. Yin Yuzhen contracts barren land in Uxin Banner and goes on to transform 70,000 mu of desert into oasis over nearly four decades.
1999 — 2017
Grain-for-Green · Beijing-Tianjin sandstorm source control
Large-scale ecological restoration programs roll out. Vegetation in sandy areas gradually recovers, laying the groundwork for the later net reduction in sandified land.
2023 — present
The Three-North battle is fully joined
The three flagship campaigns have received 88.9 billion yuan in central investment, launched 544 key projects and completed 244 million mu of work. Phase VI has assigned 463 million mu of tasks, of which 355 million mu is done.
NOV 2024
The Taklimakan Desert edge is "locked" — the final gap closed
The last 285-kilometer gap along the edge of the world's second-largest shifting desert is closed. Locking the edge does not mean planting the whole desert, but weaving a green buffer belt at its rim to stop sand from spreading outward.
2025
The belt is widened · named a Global Top 10 Engineering Achievement
21 key counties along the desert edge complete 9.3822 million mu of belt expansion, with the locked-edge width growing from 110 meters to as much as 7,500 meters. The Taklimakan edge-locking project is named one of the Top 10 Engineering Achievements of 2025 worldwide.
2026
The 14th Five-Year Plan scorecard: 15.2 million mu of sandified land treated
The National Forestry and Grassland Administration reports that sandified land continues to shrink, with wind erosion across the eight major deserts and four major sandy lands down about 40% from 2000 levels.

Covering about 42,200 km². In the early years of the People's Republic, surface vegetation cover here was just 0.9%, and over 500 million tonnes of sediment poured into the Yellow River each year. METHODOLOGY

Desert control is not just planting trees

From mechanical fixation to biological stabilization, from manual tending to smart monitoring — China has built a complete, replicable technical system. The Three-North region is divided into 136 ecological management zones, with improved seed use exceeding 75%.

STEP 01
Mechanical fixation
Straw checkerboards "pin down" the shifting sand surface, cutting near-ground wind speed and creating the conditions for vegetation to take hold.
The earliest core technique
STEP 02
Biological stabilization
Aerial seeding and drip irrigation paired with drought- and salt-tolerant native species such as Mongolian pine, sea buckthorn and cistanche build the protective system.
Survival rate: under 20% → above 75%
STEP 03
Technology-enabled
Remote sensing, drone seeding, smart irrigation, tree-planting robots and a dedicated desert-control equipment industrial park work in concert.
Efficiency up 3× · 75% mechanized
STEP 04
Model innovation
"Solar PV + sand control" has driven 5.3 million mu of treatment; 3,500+ km of desert-crossing highways have driven another 6.8 million mu.
Ecological gains and industry revenue together
Beyond technology comes institution: the central government has funded 100 dedicated "industrialization of ecology, ecologicalization of industry" projects; 10 industry standards for desertification control technology have been issued; pilot ecological compensation schemes for desert ecosystems are underway; and desert ecosystem observation stations and ground-based sandstorm monitoring stations now form an initial observation network.
CHINA'S SOLUTION

China's experience is going global

As a party to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification, China continues to deepen international cooperation on desertification control.

🌍
Africa's Great Green Wall
Chinese scientists brought water-saving irrigation technology and native plant seeds to Mauritania, building a China-Africa Green Technology Park in the Sahara that now grows Sudanese beans, eggplant and tomatoes.
🌲
Mongolia's "One Billion Trees" initiative
Chinese experts are helping promote straw checkerboard techniques and drought-tolerant planting, sharing experience in restoring arid-zone ecosystems.
⚡
Solar PV sand control in Saudi Arabia
China's "solar PV + sand control" model has been successfully deployed, easing desertification while offering a new pathway for energy transition.
🤝
China–Central Asia Desertification Control Cooperation Center
Agreed by the heads of state of the five Central Asian countries at the second China–Central Asia Summit in June 2025, the center was inaugurated in Ningxia the following month.
🏛️
Multilateral platforms
China–Central Asia, China–Arab and China–Mongolia desertification control cooperation centers have been established; ten editions of the Kubuqi International Desert Forum have been held; and the Boao Forum for Asia Ningxia desertification cooperation project is being advanced.
